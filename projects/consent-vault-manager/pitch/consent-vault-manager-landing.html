Clauses I–IV

Why a register is necessary

Any organization processing Indian personal data is now a Data Fiduciary with real, deadline-driven obligations — and no forgiveness for "we meant to track that."

I.

Consent drift

A principal revokes consent, but the processors handed their data never find out — nothing forces the withdrawal to cascade.

II.

DSR deadlines

Access, correction, and erasure requests carry statutory response windows. Miss one, and it is a violation, not an oversight.

III.

Breach notification

The Data Protection Board must be notified on a fast, defensible, provable timeline — not "as soon as someone remembers."

IV.

No audit trail

"Trust us, we handled it" does not hold up when a regulator asks for proof of exactly what happened, and when.

The Legal Foundation

Every purpose rests on exactly one basis

DPDP Act §4: processing personal data must rest on the principal's consent (§6) or one of the Act's enumerated legitimate uses (§7) — never both, never neither. This system models that as a property of the Purpose itself, not the consent record.

Consent

Requires an active, revocable ConsentArtifact — granted, tracked, and expiring on the purpose's retention schedule.

  • Marketing communications and promotional offers
  • Account creation and service delivery
  • Analytics and product improvement
Legitimate use

No consent required — or permitted. Processing proceeds on the statutory ground itself.

  • Fraud prevention and security monitoring
Enforced, not just labeled
Purpose "purpose-3" is processed under "legitimate_use" — consent cannot be recorded as its basis.

0 of 12 consent artifacts reference the legitimate-use purpose — rejected at creation, in both the mock store and the Postgres repository. Not a label anyone could forget to check: an actual constraint the code enforces every time.

Entries 01–04

Four automations, entered and running

Each one is drafted by a worker and confirmed by a person before it becomes real — see "Every entry, witnessed," below.

ENTRY 01

Consent lifecycle tracking

Grants, revocations, and expiries flow automatically. A withdrawal cascades to every downstream processor tied to that purpose.

ENTRY 02

DSR SLA monitoring

Every open Data Subject Rights request is watched against its statutory due date, with internal escalations at T‑7d and T‑2d.

ENTRY 03

Breach notice automation

A high or critical breach drafts the Data Protection Board notification and a countdown reminder for the notify-by deadline.

ENTRY 04

Weekly compliance reports

A PDF snapshot of consents, DSRs, and breaches is generated, archived, and emailed on a recurring schedule.

How automation stays safe

Every entry, witnessed

A headless worker has no Gmail or Calendar credentials, and shouldn't be handed any. So it drafts and stages — only a human, through Claude Code's live access, creates the real artifact.

STEP 1

Detect & draft

A worker notices something — an expiry, an overdue DSR, a breach — and stages a drafted email, calendar event, or report.

STEP 2

Human reviews & creates

A person reviews the draft and creates the real Gmail draft or Calendar event, through Claude Code's live connection.

STEP 3

Confirm & record

The human confirms with the real draft or event id, and the ledger records exactly what happened, with that id, permanently.

Gmail's connector here only exposes create_draft — there's no send tool. The draft sits until a human sends it: a stronger gate than anything this system adds on top.
